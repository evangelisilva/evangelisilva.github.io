About Me

I’m a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at the University at Albany, SUNY, advised by Prof. Jeong-Hyon Hwang. My research focuses on databases and distributed systems — particularly the design and optimization of on-disk and in-memory key-value stores that enable scalable and high-performance data management. My work bridges data management theory and systems engineering, with ongoing interests in storage architectures, indexing strategies, and real-time data processing frameworks.

Previously, I was a Senior Software Engineer at Sysco LABS, where I developed and optimized large-scale microservice architectures, data pipelines, and synchronization frameworks for enterprise applications. These experiences shaped my drive to build systems that are not only efficient but also resilient, observable, and impactful in real-world environments.

I’m passionate about data-intensive systems, storage research, and building tools that make data faster, smarter, and more accessible.

Projects

A Fast On-Disk Key-Value Store (Ongoing Research)

A research-driven project focused on designing a novel on-disk key-value store optimized for modern hardware and data-intensive workloads. It explores new indexing and storage techniques to achieve faster lookups and updates beyond conventional structures like B+-trees, LSM-trees, and hash-based stores.

  • Explores innovative on-disk indexing strategies for high-performance key-value access.
  • Balances throughput, latency, and space efficiency under diverse workloads.
  • Reduces write amplification and improves data locality through hybrid designs.
  • Includes empirical evaluation against state-of-the-art key-value store architectures.

This ongoing research contributes to advancing the performance boundaries of storage systems for large-scale data management.

An Experimental Evaluation and Investigation of Group Farness Estimation Techniques

A comprehensive experimental study of group farness estimation methods in large-scale graphs. While numerous algorithms exist for single-source farness (closeness) estimation, group-level estimation remains underexplored despite its significance in applications such as community detection, influence maximization, and network optimization.

  • Reviewed and analyzed existing single-source farness estimation algorithms and their applicability to group-level estimation.
  • Extended sampling-based and cardinality estimation (CE) techniques—including KMV and Cohen’s HIP permutation estimator—for scalable group farness estimation.
  • Investigated trade-offs between accuracy, memory overhead, and runtime efficiency across various sampling strategies.
  • Conducted controlled experiments comparing Cohen’s HIP-based estimator and KMV-basic, confirming Cohen’s modest yet consistent accuracy improvements (≈2–5%) and scalability advantages for large graphs.

This completed work offers quantitative insights into how sampling and cardinality estimation choices influence the accuracy and scalability of group farness estimation in large graph analytics.

Mimix Data Transformer

A modular Java library for efficient and extensible processing of Mimix event streams — enabling transformation, mapping, and post-processing workflows for relational database integration.

  • Fine-grained event filtering and JSON-based mapping configuration.
  • Supports custom transformation and post-processing hooks.
  • Pluggable architecture for extending filters, transformers, and writers.
  • Distributed as a Maven-compatible artifact for easy integration.

Use cases include real-time replication of Mimix event streams, data pipeline construction, and integration with modern data architectures.
