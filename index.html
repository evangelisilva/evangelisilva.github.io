About Me

I am a Ph.D. Student in Computer Science at the University at Albany, SUNY, advised by Prof. Jeong-Hyon Hwang. My research focuses on database and storage systems, particularly on the design and optimization of on-disk index structures and memory-efficient caching. I aim to build scalable, high-performance data infrastructures that support next-generation data-intensive workloads.

Prior to joining UAlbany, I worked as a Senior Software Engineer at Sysco LABS, where I developed and scaled distributed microservice systems and optimized data architectures.

Research and Projects

Doctoral Research

University at Albany, SUNY (Jan 2022 – Present)

On-Disk Index Design and Evaluation

  • Investigating an alternative on-disk indexing approach intended to reduce page-management overhead and improve memory utilization for key-value storage.
  • Developed a benchmarking framework with common index interfaces, workload generators, and performance measurements to support fair and reproducible comparisons.
  • Conducted preliminary evaluations of B+-trees, LSM-trees, and Bf-Tree across dataset scales, key/value configurations, memory budgets, insertion orders, access patterns, workload types, and storage media.
  • Analyzing operation latency and storage I/O behavior to characterize performance trade-offs; obtained preliminary results indicating potential benefits of the proposed approach.
  • Preparing a benchmark manuscript describing the framework and preliminary findings.

An Experimental Evaluation of Group Farness Estimation Techniques

  • Conducted an empirical study of scalable group farness estimation in large graphs, extending single-source algorithms to multi-node group settings.
  • Evaluated sampling and cardinality estimation methods, including KMV and Cohen's HIP, to analyze accuracy, memory consumption, and runtime trade-offs.
